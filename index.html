How it works

PROOF in three steps

Pick the form, fill in six parts, certify. The same record travels to the next relationship.

  1. 01

    Pick the form

    Assessed once, or carries on. That is the only question. A single investment or closing takes PROOF-T; an LP, client or portfolio company takes PROOF-O. Any entity or trust attaches PROOF-E.

  2. 02

    Fill in six parts

    The matter, the parties, the origin of funds, screening, reports considered. Facts and evidence, not clauses. Every field is filled, and a blank is not an answer.

  3. 03

    Certify and hand it over

    Part 6 is a fixed certification. It is the part that makes the form worth something to the person receiving it, and it is the part nobody redlines.

Why PROOF

Four properties, one standard.

A standard form is not adopted because it is well drafted. It is adopted because of four properties, and dropping any one of them is what kills the others.

01

A form, not a framework

You provide facts and evidence. You do not draft clauses or negotiate wording.

02

Standardised

The same core format across funds and asset managers, rather than rebuilt for every onboarding. No options matrix, no "select applicable subsections".

03

Free and open

Apache 2.0. No licence fee, no gate, no account.

04

Not designed to be redlined

The value of a standard disappears if every organisation creates its own version. That is the entire point, and it is rule 13.

The problem

The seed round legal bill of private markets

Talk to fund managers, asset managers, compliance teams and lawyers across the United States and the same frustration comes up.

What it looks like today

KYC is governed by a patchwork of legal requirements, regulatory expectations and industry practice. That patchwork has never produced a clear, widely accepted standard for how KYC should be collected, evidenced and documented. So every fund, asset manager, administrator and adviser spends time building and maintaining its own version of essentially the same process.

What PROOF does instead

Do for KYC what standardised documents such as the YC SAFE did for early stage financing. Instead of every organisation creating its own version of the same process, establish a common format that everyone can understand and use: a consistent set of information, evidence and certifications that moves with a party from one relationship to the next.

What PROOF standardises

How core information, ownership details, origin of funds evidence, screening results and supporting documentation are collected and recorded. It applies in both directions: the LPs whose capital you accept, and the companies you send it to. The record stays consistent even when the fund, manager, administrator or adviser changes.

What PROOF does not do

It does not replace a fund's or asset manager's legal or compliance judgement. Each manager remains responsible for deciding what diligence is required, how risk is assessed, and whether a party is accepted. Whether to take the money, or send it, is the manager's call on the manager's own responsibility. It is not legal advice.

Every PROOF form includes

  • The matter: what is being assessed, once or on an ongoing basis, and which annex applies.
  • The parties: every natural person, entity and trust, with PROOF-E attached for each entity.
  • The origin of funds, with the evidence relied on.
  • Screening results, with the reports considered and a reason wherever a report was not required.
  • A fixed certification. The circumstances may change; the underlying KYC record should not.

Scope, what actually binds in the United States, and the machine readable layer are set out in the background. The practice note works through what diligence in both directions looks like in practice. The thirteen rules take one minute to read.
